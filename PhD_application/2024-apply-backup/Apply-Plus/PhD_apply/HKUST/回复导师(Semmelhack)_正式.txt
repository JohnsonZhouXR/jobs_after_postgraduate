Thank you for your kind response and for the opportunity to elaborate on my research interests and aspirations for a PhD. I am deeply motivated to contribute to the field of Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI), and I would like to outline my thoughts and plans below.

In the early stages of my PhD, I aim to gain a solid understanding of signal processing techniques in both non-invasive and invasive BCI technologies. Specifically, I would like to explore non-invasive methods such as EEG, ECoG, MEG, and fMRI, as well as invasive approaches like intracortical microelectrodes and Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS).

Building on this foundation, I plan to conduct experiments in the early to mid-phases of my PhD. Having some prior familiarity with the OpenBCI library, I propose acquiring equipment (within a budget of 10,000 HKD) to collect human brain signals for analysis. One observation I have made is that while many current studies focus on analyzing EEG signals, relatively few explore providing meaningful feedback to the brain. To address this, I propose improving OpenBCI’s non-invasive electrodes—typically worn on the scalp—to enable them to generate temperature changes. These changes could provide feedback to the brain through skin sensations, potentially enabling a novel form of closed-loop interaction.

If this concept proves viable, I would begin with simple tasks, such as controlling a switch or a robotic gripper—essentially 1-bit tasks. These would resemble classification problems in neural networks, similar to a project I worked on where I classified facial gender into binary outputs (male or female).

Building on the insights from these 1-bit tasks, I would gradually increase the complexity of the outputs, progressing to 8-bit tasks to address moderately complex objectives, such as controlling a robotic arm (initially with fewer degrees of freedom).

This progression would allow me to explore the capabilities and limitations of non-invasive technologies, identify ways to optimize electrode designs, and determine the extent to which non-invasive techniques can support practical applications. If significant limitations arise, I would transition to exploring invasive technologies based on the groundwork laid in earlier experiments.

In this advanced phase, I would aim to learn how to collaborate effectively with surgeons to ensure ethical, hygienic, and safe experimental practices.

Ultimately, my goal is to integrate artificial intelligence and computer science techniques to better interpret and analyze brain signals while developing innovative feedback mechanisms for the brain. Through this work, I hope to deepen our understanding of the brain and contribute to advancing neuroscience and BCI technologies.
