During the early phase of my PhD, I aim to deepen my understanding of signal processing techniques in Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) technologies. This includes exploring non-invasive methods such as EEG, ECoG, MEG, and fMRI, as well as invasive techniques like intracortical microelectrodes and Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS).

Building on this foundational knowledge, I plan to initiate experimental work in the early to mid-stages of my PhD. I have some prior familiarity with the OpenBCI library, and I propose acquiring equipment (with a budget not exceeding 10,000 HKD) to collect human brain signals for analysis. I have noticed that while many studies focus on analyzing EEG signals, relatively few explore providing feedback to the brain. To address this, I am considering improving OpenBCI’s non-invasive electrodes (typically worn on the scalp) to enable them to generate temperature variations. This feedback mechanism, sensed through the skin, could deliver signals back to the brain.

If this idea proves viable, I would like to start with simple tasks, such as controlling a switch or opening and closing a robotic gripper—essentially, 1-bit tasks. These tasks resemble neural network classification problems, akin to a project I previously worked on where I classified facial gender into binary outputs (male or female).

Based on the experience gained from 1-bit tasks, I would gradually increase the complexity of the outputs, progressing to 8-bit tasks for slightly more sophisticated objectives, such as controlling a robotic arm (initially with a limited number of axes).

Through this process, I aim to explore the capabilities and limitations of non-invasive technologies for signal processing and identify potential electrode optimizations. If the signal processing limitations of non-invasive methods become a significant bottleneck, I would consider transitioning to experiments involving invasive techniques.

In this phase, I would focus on learning how to collaborate effectively with surgeons and address basic hygiene and safety requirements to ensure ethical and responsible experimentation.

Ultimately, my goal is to leverage artificial intelligence and computer science techniques to better interpret and analyze brain signals while also developing innovative feedback mechanisms for the brain. By bridging neuroscience with computational methods, I hope to contribute to advancing BCI technologies and expanding our understanding of brain functionality.
